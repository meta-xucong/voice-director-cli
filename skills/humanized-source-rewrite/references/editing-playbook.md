# Editing playbook: reader, genre, and Chinese prose cues

Use these as editorial prompts, not automatic rules. A pattern needs revision only when it obscures meaning, sounds wrong for the reader, or repeats without useful function.

## Reader and purpose

- What is the reader/listener trying to understand, decide, feel, or do?
- What does the source support, and which detail would make the explanation clearer without adding unsupported content?
- Prefer the actual answer over a throat-clearing opening. Ask a question only when it reflects a real reader question or advances reasoning.

## Chinese prose cues

| Possible cue | Ask | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract official/business verbs such as “赋能、打造、闭环、抓手、聚焦” | Who does what, to which process/person, with what evidenced result? | Keep a term when it is the correct technical or organizational term. |
| Broad praise such as “全面提升、意义重大、开启新篇章” | Is a concrete result, evidence, or decision needed? | Keep a justified or attributed evaluation. |
| Hidden actors/actions in nominalized or passive language | Can the supported actor and action be stated directly? | Do not assign responsibility the source does not establish. |
| Repeated roadmap/signposts | Does this signpost help navigation or restate what is already clear? | Keep signposts that clarify complex structure. |
| Forced parallelism or repeated “不是……而是……” | Is the structure logically necessary or made overly symmetrical? | Preserve real contrasts, canonical definitions, and deliberate rhetoric. |
| Generic openings, filler transitions, repeated recap | Does the line add a relevant question, relation, evidence, or reminder? | Keep transitions that explain a genuine shift in topic or logic. |
| Uniform sentence/paragraph rhythm | Would the idea benefit from a different sentence length or pause? | Do not add random fragments to simulate humanity. |
| Translation-like phrasing or excessive connectors | Would a native speaker say it more directly? | Retain connectors needed for technical reasoning or clarity. |
| Fake intimacy or fabricated human texture | Is the warmth grounded in the author's real stance or source facts? | Do not add personal memories, “我亲测,” or audience reactions without support. |

## Maturity and handoff

- **Voice boundary:** Keep evidence, confidence labels, and editorial notes outside clean copy. Ensure standalone copy works without hidden source context.
- **Repetition function:** Inspect neighboring and distant passages. Preserve useful recall, emphasis, navigation, contrast, rhythm, or examples; merge only accidental duplication after mapping information back to source.
- **Claim integrity:** Distinguish source claim, transcription repair, paraphrase, editorial inference, and factual correction. Keep wording no stronger than evidence.
- **Delivery:** Narration should be speakable and terminology-consistent without stage directions or unresolved editor notes.
- **Separate verdicts:** Coverage, claim/attribution integrity, audience/genre fit, repetition, clean-copy readiness, and unresolved evidence are distinct review axes.

## Genre adapter: teaching narration

Help the learner understand a concept, distinction, or procedure. Explain confusing steps and use examples only when sourced or explicitly requested. Check for too many rhetorical questions, repeated “记住/我们来看,” fake classroom intimacy, and unnecessary recaps. Retain the technical precision and meaningful repetition required for learning.

When a voice sample is supplied, infer only formality, directness, rhythm, vocabulary, explanation depth, and warmth. Keep the sample's facts and distinctive wording out of the rewrite unless they are also in the source. Do not optimize against AI detectors; give concrete editorial observations instead of a score.
